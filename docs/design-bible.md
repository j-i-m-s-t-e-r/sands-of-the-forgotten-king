# SANDS OF THE FORGOTTEN KING
### A Text Adventure — Design Bible v0.1

---

## 1. PREMISE

**Setting:** Cairo and the Western Desert, 1936.
**Player:** An Egyptologist (freelance, formerly attached to a museum that cut your funding after a "reckless" prior expedition — good backstory hook for NPCs to reference). **Surname: Pembroke** — established via the stencilled name on the trunk in the hotel room (Act I). First name not yet fixed; decide if/when it needs to appear on-page (the Act V inspiration draft used "Arthur" as a placeholder — not yet adopted as canon).

**Premise refinement (REVISED):** Pembroke is a freelance Egyptologist whom the Trustees of the British Museum cut loose after a "reckless" earlier expedition. He was in London, between jobs, when Whitfield's telegram summoned him to Cairo; he read it, booked passage, and arrived at Ravenwood Hotel (booked for him by Whitfield) the night before the game begins. Whitfield is the Trustees' real man in Egypt: officially a scholar cataloguing at the Museum Annex, in practice someone whose whole career has been "acquiring" antiquities for the Museum, which is to say stealing them, under the cover of scholarship. **Whitfield was the one originally sent to find the pyramid.** He got into trouble with the Cairo police (a crate with rather more opium in it than the manifest said), cannot leave the city, and has asked Pembroke to come and finish the job in his place, handing over everything he knows at the Annex. The job is also Pembroke's way back into the Trustees' good books. This matters for tone: Pembroke is picking up someone else's mission mid-crisis, and the mentor who hands it over is, gently, a crook.

**Tone:** Indiana Jones — dusty, wry, a little dangerous, respectful of the mythology rather than cartoonish. NPCs should feel like real people with their own agendas (the dealer wants money, the scholar wants credit denied to him by history, the Bedouin guide wants safe passage guaranteed).

**Thematic spine (important for internal consistency):** *Ma'at* — balance, truth, the "weighing of the heart." This isn't just flavor; it's mechanically load-bearing:
- Referenced first casually (Act 1, scholar's house).
- Reinforced literally (Act 4, Weighing Room puzzle — you must balance a scale to pass, using knowledge not treasure).
- Paid off at the climax (Act 5 — the lift at [39], with the Djinn as its voice, administers a final "weighing." A heart weighed down by greed/treasure cannot leave. You must exit empty-handed and unburdened, mirroring the exact mechanic from Act 4.)

This means **the Weighing Room puzzle in Act 4 is not optional flavor — it's foreshadowing we must not cut.**

- **[1] Bazaar Entrance** is the hub. All Act I locations connect back to it directly or in 1 hop.

### ACT II — DESERT (locations 11–16, linear; [14] is now on the path)
```
[11] Desert Road → [12] Oasis Rest Stop → [13] Shifting Dunes → [14] Bedouin Camp → [15] Sandstorm Ridge → [16] Valley of Forgotten Stones
```
- [13] Shifting Dunes is a **navigation puzzle**: throw date stones, beetles reveal bad ground (taught in advance at [12]).
- [14] Bedouin Camp is **required** because the fortune-teller gives the flute needed at [35]. The tea and tarot trades there remain optional and independent. (This replaces the earlier "optional branch" design.)
- Nothing in Act II is unwinnable: the always-available N,N,E,E route at [15] still needs no prep.

### ACT III — PYRAMID UPPER (locations 17–22, linear until the trap)
```
[16]→ [17] Buried Entrance Stairway → [18] Antechamber of Warnings
        → [19] Collapsed Threshold (DOOR SEALS HERE — point of no return)
        → [20] Hall of Offerings → [21] Guardian Statues Corridor → [22] Well Shaft (Down)
```
- **[19] is the curse trigger.** Player is warned twice before this (once by the scholar in [10], once by hieroglyphs in [18]) so it reads as "I was told and did it anyway" — classic pulp-hero recklessness, not a cheap gotcha.
- [22] Well Shaft is the sole connector down into Act IV.

- Design rule: every one of the 15 rooms should be reachable, and no single required item should be missable in a way that soft-locks the game. We'll track this explicitly in the Puzzle Dependency Graph (Section 5) as we build each room.

**Prices.** The critical path stays affordable without the fez (50 piastres covers amulet + camel). Wearing the fez gives "local price".

| Amulet | 20 | 12 |
| Camel | 25 | 15 |
| Knife repair at the blacksmith | 10 | 6 |
| Tea at the tea stall | 8 | 5 |

The jacket holds nothing; the mirror text changes to shirtsleeves afterwards. Worn at the Oasis [12], the sunglasses cut the water's glare so the larvae show; a player who drinks the water unprotected dies. |
| Blacksmith | Repairs the broken knife (10 / 6 with fez). **The knife comes out of the briefcase when it is opened at the start of the game**: a Swiss army penknife engraved with the crest of the University of Manchester, snapped at the hinge, "broken, but not beyond repair". It is no longer found at the docks. The knife is **optional**: it cuts ropes in the pyramid (the rope holding the blade at [26], and the net at [22]) and `WAVE KNIFE` deters the Bazaar pickpocket. The blacksmith says "a knife shows thieves you are no fool". |

**Bazaar pickpocket (built).** The merchant warns "watch your pockets, effendi". On entering the Bazaar with the watch, a thin man drifts closer; if the player is still there two commands later he takes the pocketwatch. Leaving the Bazaar cancels it. `SHOW`, `WAVE`, `HOLD`, `POINT` or `USE KNIFE` (repaired) all deter him. The watch is recovered from the thief's body at [13].

**Layout (approved):** Tea Merchant's Stall west of the Tea House, Rug Dealer north of the Bazaar, Blacksmith south of the Bazaar, Docks south of the Camel Market.

## 11. PRODUCTION PIVOT — BUILDING THIS AS SOFTWARE, NOT JUST A DOCUMENT

Decision: stop expanding the design document indefinitely and get a **playable vertical slice** working first, then grow outward from there. Big documents are a good map; they're not a game.

**Approach:**
- **Engine stays "boring" and generic** — it should know nothing about Egypt specifically. Just rooms, objects, NPCs, flags, a parser, and (later) save/load. That means later projects (a pirate game, etc.) reuse the same engine — only the data changes.
- **Content is data-driven** — rooms/objects/NPCs/dialogue live in a data file, not hardcoded into engine logic. Keeps the engine stable while 95% of future effort goes into writing rooms, puzzles, and dialogue.
- **Vertical slice first:** the 8 Cairo locations that form a complete, self-contained mini-arc (learn the pyramid's location, hire a camel, leave the city) — playable start to finish before we touch the other 32 rooms.
- **Token/context discipline:** once the engine is stable, we shouldn't need to touch engine code again — most future sessions should just be "here's a new room" / "rewrite this dialogue," not re-litigating parser mechanics.

## 13. THE MAT & LIFT MECHANISM (Ma'at, made literal)

This is the single biggest piece of long-range design in the game — two rooms, dozens apart, connected by one physical object, and the player has to notice the connection themselves for the ending to land.

**[17] Buried Entrance Stairway — the Weighing Mat.** The very first thing that happens on entering the pyramid, before the player has picked up a single piece of treasure. Rows of bamboo canes, weak to strong, left to right. The player crosses; weaker canes crack until they reach ones thick enough to hold. Red sand drains through the gap at that exact point and is automatically captured in a glass — no verb, no puzzle input, pure atmosphere. This is the pyramid honestly weighing the player before greed enters the picture. Not referenced again until [39].

**[39] Threshold of Judgment — the lift (REVISED this session; supersedes the earlier cargo/strand design).** The glass jar of red sand captured at the mat [17] reappears here as the visible balance target. The rule is thematic, not arithmetic: **the player must drop all stolen treasure** (anything taken from the pyramid and tagged `TREASURE`). What they carried into the pyramid (kitbag contents, tools) is irrelevant, so there is no inventory-snapshot or exact-weight mechanic. Outcomes:

| Situation | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Player on the lift, all stolen treasure dropped | **Success.** The lift rises smoothly and balances against the jar, carrying the player to the true ending (§10). |
| Player on the lift, still carrying stolen treasure | **Failure.** The lift rises too far, the rope runs out, and the player falls and dies: the same game-over screen as the pearl trial (restart or load a save). |
| Treasure on the lift, player not on it | The lift does not rise. Safe, no penalty. (The earlier "treasure rides up alone and strands the player" death is cancelled.) |

**Physical-logic flag:** a heavier load making a lift rise further is backwards if the sand jar is a plain counterweight, and this project's rule is logical, sensible solutions. Suggested fix, no change to outcomes: show the pointer overshooting a marked line as load is added, and describe the mechanism as a governed ratchet that releases too much rope under an overload. The player then reads "pointer past the mark = bad" before ever committing.

**What counts, and how the player can tell (confirmed): the pyramid sign.** All treasure that must be dropped carries a small pyramid mark, showing it is property of the pyramid. Anything marked must be dropped before the lift. Small tools (a lamp, a key, a pickaxe, the flute, the hook) carry no mark and may be kept. **The mark is revealed by `EXAMINE <object>`** (the object's description states whether it is marked); it is not shown automatically in room or inventory listings, and it applies only to treasures that must be dropped, never to tools. Implementation: a `marked: true` property on items, with the mark line added to the examine text; the lift counts marked items carried.

**Fairness risk, and mitigations to build in:** because the mark only shows on examine, a player who never examines their loot could ride the lift carrying marked items. Keep this fair with redundant cues that need no examining: the Djinn names "the mark of the house" the first time it taxes the player; a carving at the lift shows the mark and a heap of dropped loot; the orbs show their pyramid of light openly.

**Marked items so far:** gold, jewels and relics generally; the Rosetta Stone; the pharaoh's mask; the four orbs from the pillar wing (each with a small pyramid of light floating at its centre, so it is unmistakably marked). **Unmarked (allowed):** lamp, key, hook, pickaxe, flute, kitbag contents. **The swallowed pearl** is a gift from the Djinn, earned by choosing the smallest, so it should carry no mark (proposed, not yet confirmed).

**Subtle foreshadowing of "stolen treasure" is required throughout the pyramid phase** (confirmed). Candidate cues, to be approved: the Djinn's "Theft never pays" and its tax lines; carved hieroglyphs of thieves being weighed; Voss's notes; ghost remarks; a visible heap of dropped loot near the lift. The word the game uses should be "stolen" or "taken", never "carried", so the player attaches the rule to theft, not weight.

**Swallowed pearl and the lift (confirmed):** the pearl from [31] is internalized, so the lift treats it as part of the player. It adds no tracked weight, never needs to be dropped, and never counts as cargo. Don't build a margin-of-error mechanic for it; the rule is simply that swallowed means yours.

**Grace turns (approved):** after the player steps on, the lift waits two turns before moving; `GET OFF` in that window cancels it. A pointer past the marked line (carrying marked treasure) comes with a loud groan.

**Implementation note:** the engine needs an "on the lift" state for the player (`GET ON LIFT` / `GET OFF`) and a count of stolen-treasure items carried. `DROP` while on the lift just leaves the item on the floor, not as cargo.

**Fail-state taxonomy across the dungeon (for consistency as we build more rooms):** the dungeon now has three distinct severity tiers, and it's worth keeping them distinct on purpose rather than blurring them:
- **Tier 1 — mild (no loss):** [35] flute puzzle, wrong note → reset one step back. Nothing lost.
- **Tier 3b — hard death:** [39] lift failure (player riding with stolen treasure): the rope runs out, the player falls, game-over screen. Same severity as the pearl trial.
- **Tier 3 — hard death (four lethal points in total):** the Oasis water [12], the blade trap at [26] (confirmed hard death), the lift [39] and [31] pearl trial. Each must be taught before it can kill. [31] pearl trial → fatal for Voss and for the player: game-over screen, then restart or load a save.

## 14. THE DJINN & HIEROGLYPH HINT SYSTEM

Every hieroglyph-bearing puzzle in the dungeon can be solved unaided, by observation and trial-and-error — the game's fairness rule (no pure-luck puzzles) still holds. But solving one unaided while carrying any treasure has a cost: **the Djinn manifests, takes one piece of treasure as a "tax," says something like "No free lunches, even if you are lucky," and vanishes back into the wall.** If the player is carrying no treasure at the time, there's no tax — instead, a dry warning: *"Theft never pays."* The Djinn never punishes poverty, only greed.

The **Rosetta Stone** ([21], see location table) is the counter to this: worn around the neck, it passively translates hieroglyphs into direct hints, no verb needed, and exempts the player from the tax entirely. It's valuable without being mandatory — exactly the balance the fairness rule requires.

This is the same Djinn who runs the pearl trial at [31] — confirmed as one recurring character, not a generic mechanic repeating itself. That's a deliberate choice: it makes the whole dungeon feel like it's being watched and tested continuously by a single, consistent presence, not just at two big set-pieces.

Rooms confirmed to need this system so far: [18] Antechamber of Warnings, [27] Hall of Mirrors. More will be added as further Act IV rooms get designed.

### Sandstorm Ridge — full mechanism

The most mechanically complex room in Act II, offering two independent, non-luck-based ways through, layered by how much Act II prep the player did.

**The two mirages** (both presented as if genuine exits; following either loops the player back to the ridge — wasted time, never fatal):
- Camels approaching in the distance — actually just dune-shadow.
- A beautiful woman about to enter a shimmering pool — not real.

**Behavior by preparation state:**

| Player has | What happens |
|---|---|
| Nothing (no tea, no advice) | Both mirages are presented as tempting, plausible paths. Following either loops back. The only real solution is the N,N,E,E fallback below — found by elimination, not luck. |
| Mint tea only | Mirages still appear, but are narrated as recognizably false (the player isn't fooled, doesn't need to test them) — still has to find N,N,E,E themselves. |
| Fortune-teller's advice only | Knows to open the umbrella ("follow the wind") for an immediate shortcut, regardless of whether the mirages would have fooled them. |
| Both | Easiest run — mirages are transparent, and the umbrella shortcut is known. |

**The fallback (always available, no prep required):** N, N, E, E, through a stretch of dunes deliberately described as featureless — the "boring" option next to two flashy false leads. Discoverable by a careful player who refuses to chase the mirages and tries plain compass directions instead.

**The umbrella shortcut:** opening the umbrella here lets the wind carry the player directly to [16] — and immediately whisks the umbrella away.

## 16. THE PILLAR WING (new, about 50-room expansion)

Below the pyramid's main network is a group of four huge obsidian pillars, once a mystic energy source and now dormant. Each wakes when a glowing orb is placed at its base.

- **The orbs:** found by examining sarcophagi. The mummies inside are holding them. Each orb is visibly different and it is obvious which pillar it belongs to, so there is no guessing: this is a fair, observation-based puzzle that follows the house style.
- **Reward:** with all four active, a figure of the final pharaoh appears and says, telepathically, "I will see you soon." (Designer's word was "holograph"; that is a tech term and must not appear in game text. See the ambiguity rule below.)
- **Orbs are TREASURE until placed (confirmed):** taking an orb from a mummy's hands is theft in the game's moral logic. An orb still in the player's inventory when they ride the lift [39] counts as stolen treasure and they fall to their death like any other. Once placed at a pillar it is left behind and no longer counts. So the optional wing carries a real trap: a player who grabs orbs and never completes the pillars must drop them before the lift. Warning is built in: each orb has a small pyramid of light floating at its centre, the same pyramid mark that stamps all treasure (§13). Wording for the in-game text: "a small pyramid of light", never "hologram".
- **Ambiguity rule (from §10):** obsidian pillars as a dormant energy source lean toward science fiction. Keep it unexplained. Never say alien, technology, or hologram; the player may read it as magic, machine or something else, exactly as with the Pharaoh himself.
- **Continuity:** "I will see you soon" foreshadows the Pharaoh's opening line in the true ending, "You took longer than I expected." They should read as the same voice and the same patience.

**Size (decided): 8 rooms:** an approach, two puzzle chambers, the pillar hall, and four tomb chambers (one per orb).

**Scale and atmosphere (decided this session):**
- The player goes **down, down, down, down**: a long descent that tells the player how deep this goes before they see anything.
- At the bottom, the pillars are **about 400 ft tall** when the player looks up from their base. The base is a **circular path about 100 metres around**.
- A **hum, glow and warmth** fill the place, so **no lamp is needed here**. (The lamp's flame looks pale beside the glow.)
- The point of every description is **latent, immense power**, dormant but not dead. Cues to use: a hum felt in the chest more than heard; warmth like a banked furnace; a slow pulse in the glow like sleeping breath; the lamp flame leaning toward the pillars; an unplaceable smell like the air before a storm; no visible top, only light thinning into the dark. Keep it unexplained: never say hologram, alien, technology or machine (see the ambiguity rule above).

**Structure (decided, revised this session; 8 rooms, no puzzles in the wing itself):**
1. **The Long Descent** — one room, four stages. Each `DOWN` advances a counter and shows new text (down, down, down, down), so the player feels the depth without four rooms.
2. **Two more descent rooms** (invented text, no puzzles) to carry the descent. These are the former spare rooms.
3. **Four pillar-base rooms** around the circular path (about 100 m round): the four locations that receive an orb as a "key". Each has a towering pillar and a socket at its base.
4. **One central room**, where the pharaoh's figure appears when all four pillars are active.
Total 3 + 4 + 1 = 8. The wing is a place of awe and payoff, with no puzzles beyond placing the orbs. Because the wing is lit by the pillars, it is the one place the lamp is unnecessary.
**The four sarcophagi and orbs (decided):** they are spread across rooms elsewhere in the pyramid. A sarcophagus can't be moved, only opened (`OPEN SARCOPHAGUS`, then `GET ORB` from the mummy's hands). **Two of the four are roped and sealed shut, and only the penknife will cut the ropes.** Consequence: a player without the repaired knife can collect only two orbs and cannot wake all four pillars, so full completion needs the optional Cairo chain (pen to fez to cheaper knife repair; see Cairo expansion). **The four rooms (approved):** [20] Hall of Offerings (open; the first mummy and the first orb, teaching the pyramid-of-light mark early and safely), [25] Snake Pit (roped, in an optional room), [28] Minor Noble's Burial Chamber (open, on the way to the mask), and [36] Chamber of Echoes (roped, behind the hollow wall the player finds by listening). Constraints: collect any orb before the one-way swing at [29] if its room is on the near side; carried orbs are marked treasure, so the Djinn tax applies without the Stone (the [20] orb is taken before the Stone is found).

**Decided:** the wing is optional (extra score and lore; the game can be finished without it). It is reached through the Collapsed Tunnel [33] once that is dug out, so [33]'s dig-out puzzle is now the gateway. **Not yet decided:** how [33] is dug out, how many rooms the wing contains (pillar hall plus the sarcophagus chambers, probably 6 to 10), and whether the pyramid mark is shown on every marked item from the first treasure onward (recommended).

## 17. TEXT STYLE AND SMALL ROOM DETAILS (decided this session)

**Voice.** Second person, present tense. Dry, wry, period-British understatement, with Pembroke earnest and slightly out of his depth. At most one joke per room; dread stays serious.

**Length and format.** Three to five sentences per room. Dialogue in quotes. Score messages stay as `[+10 points — reason]`. **Room descriptions always print in full** on every visit, as the current build does (no brief mode).

**Sensory rule (decided; applies to every act).** Every room description must give **smell, sound and sight**, plus the heat or cold where it matters. Every `EXAMINE` of a physical object must give its **touch** (hot, cold, glassy, slippery, rough, old, immaculate, dusty, wet and so on). The atmosphere is sold through the senses. Existing Cairo text has been rewritten to this standard; Acts II to V must be written to it from the start.

**Character appearance rule (decided).** Every character gets one notable physical feature, so no two are alike. **Forbidden words in game text:** alien, hologram, technology, machine. The pillars and the Pharaoh stay unexplained.

**Presentation.** Amber text on black, text only: no art, no sound, a simple title screen. Delivered as an HTML5 build for itch.io.

**Death screens (four lethal points).** A short, wry epitaph from a sardonic narrator, then the game-over screen with restart or load. A different line for each death.
- **[26] blade trap (decided, your line):** "Ancient proverb: Protect ya neck."

**Five changes after a play review:**
1. **The dunes now teach, and punish, the same way.** The cue is in the room itself: with date stones the description says "the vendor's lesson comes back to you: a thrown stone shows you what the sand will do before your boot has to"; with none it says "You have nothing to test the ground with. The vendor at the oasis spent a date stone on sand just like this." It also warns that "nobody who steps out untried comes back to say so". Only ground that has been tested (a stone thrown and left untouched) can be stepped onto. Any untested step, safe or not, makes the slope let go: the player sinks ("ankle, knee, waist, chest... the light turning brown, then black"), blacks out, and comes to coughing sand on the Desert Road beside the camel, with nothing lost but the walk. Testing a slope already known to be bad costs no further stone, so the six stones can never run out.
2. **The Pharaoh on Voss:** "Voss was another thief. A clever thief, but in the end, a thief."
3. **A wrong Serpent Bridge door sends the player to the Antechamber of Warnings** (no bite, no Snake Pit): "The mosaic floor does the opposite of holding... Somewhere above you a voice like a cello is laughing... and as you fall the laughter thins and frays at the edges and turns into a hiss, a dry, mocking approximation of a snake: sss, sss-sss-sss." The penalty is the long walk back (Threshold, Hall, the statue corridor with its three tiles again, the Well Shaft, down and across), which was tested and is open.
4. **`DROP ALL` in the garden, the Lift Antechamber and the lift also takes off and drops marked things the player is wearing** (the mask and the Stone), each scoring as it falls. Elsewhere, `DROP ALL` drops everything except what the player will need and *names* what it keeps: "You keep Penknife, Waterskin, Reed Flute, because you will need them yet. If you are quite sure, DROP ALL again." Items that cannot be dropped for another reason are listed by name with their reason.
5. **`DROP TREASURE`** (also `DROP ALL TREASURE`, `DROP MARKED`, `DROP LOOT`, and the verb `SURRENDER`) drops every marked thing the player has, taking off worn items first, anywhere. Away from the surrender areas it asks for confirmation before giving up the Stone or the mask: "You keep Funerary Mask, Rosetta Stone, because you will need them yet. If you are quite sure, DROP TREASURE again."

**The sensory rule, extended to five senses (set for the refinement pass):** every room description should feed sight, smell, sound and, where it fits, taste; every EXAMINE should include touch. Sight is always present. A keyword scan of the build at the time (indicative only, it misses unusual wording) gave this baseline: **touch** is strong (45 of 51 rooms, 119 of 125 examinable objects; the six without are the hole, the blade's rope, the lintels, the grapnel, the pointer and the map of Cairo); **sound** is good (43 of 51 rooms; missing from the Statue Corridor, Burial Chamber, Serpent Bridge, Treasury, Collapsed Tunnel, Long Descent, Lift Antechamber and the Platform); **smell** is fair (33 of 51 rooms, with gaps in the rug dealer, blacksmith, dunes, camp, ridge, stairway, threshold, mummy's chamber, tunnel and most of the pillar wing; every character has one); **taste** is by far the weakest: 11 of 51 rooms (mostly "the air tastes of...") and only 2 of 125 objects. Ideas to try: a `TASTE` or `LICK` verb with a per-object taste line (and a few lethal or funny ones: the pool, the black water); warm leather from the waterskin; the grit of the dunes; tea, dates, mint and clove in Cairo and the camp; lime dust and old metal in the pyramid, but sparingly, since "the air tastes of" is already used in a dozen rooms.

**The hotel is the Ravenwood Hotel** (it was Shepheard's, the real Cairo hotel), a nod to *Raiders of the Lost Ark*, where Marion Ravenwood's family name belongs to the adventure's heroine. It appears as "Ravenwood Hotel — Your Room" in the game, and the telegram now ends "ROOM BOOKED RAVENWOOD STOP".

**Whitfield briefs the player in one conversation (found in play):** getting the supplies required knowing to `ASK` him about them, which nothing in the game tells you. Now the first `TALK TO WHITFIELD` is the whole briefing: his greeting ("Don't sit, there is no time. Listen."), the pyramid and Kasim (with the magnifying glass, +10 learned about Kasim), then the story of the police and the crate and the hand-over of the kit-bag and the umbrella (+10 received supplies). If the player had already asked about one of them, only the missing part is delivered. On later visits he says "Pembroke. Still here? You ought to be gone. What more can I tell you?" and the topics (pyramid, Voss, supplies, Trustees) remain available. The scoring is unchanged.

**The Tea House is now the Coffee House (two places selling tea was confusing):** the room is "The Coffee House" (internal id unchanged), with scorched cardamom in the air and a boy carrying "tiny cups, each holding something black and slow as tar". `BUY COFFEE`, `GET COFFEE` or `DRINK COFFEE` costs 2 piastres (1 with the fez) and says: "The coffee is marginally more fluid than tar, and somehow tastes worse: bitter, burnt and gritty, with a sweetness that arrives too late to help. Hassan's topic is now coffee ("I would not, myself, but the boy will"), his first-meeting line says Voss "drank my coffee for a week", the map of Cairo reads COFFEE HOUSE (realigned), and the wares list says "Cup of coffee".

**Hassan on Voss, and on Kasim (found in play):** asking Hassan about Voss answered "Ask me about something I'd actually know", and so did asking about Kasim. Now `ASK HASSAN ABOUT VOSS` (or `THE GERMAN`) gets: "The German? Speaks lies in many languages. He was here about two weeks ago. He probably knows something you don't. Whitfield in the museum won't talk to him." And asking about Kasim, the antiquities market or a gift gives the same rumour as asking about the pyramid (Kasim trusts gifts, not strangers; +10 the first time), since a player who has just heard Kasim's name from Whitfield will naturally ask. Voss is added to Hassan's suggested topics.

**What the teabags are for is now stated (found in play):** a player at the Tea Stall asked about tea, examined the teabags, tried to give them to the seller, tried to use them, and never learned their purpose, because the hint only fired on the word "teabags". Now: (1) `ASK`/`TALK` about *tea* while carrying the teabags makes the Tea Seller notice them at once: "But what is that I smell? English tea! Rubbish, effendi, I would not serve it to a goat. But the Bedouin, out in the desert beyond the dunes, are mad for it. Take it to them, and they will trade you a glass of their own mint tea: the real thing, which clears a man's head even in a sandstorm." (2) Giving him the teabags or the kit-bag explains instead of refusing: "Not for me, effendi. I am a man of mint and cardamom. Keep it. Out in the desert beyond the dunes, the Bedouin would trade you the real thing for it..." (3) `USE TEABAGS` says nobody here has a kettle or time, and that "somebody out in the desert will want these a great deal more than you do." (4) `GET TEABAGS` (or anything already carried) now says the item is already in your kit-bag, instead of "You don't see that here". (5) `GIVE <item>` with no recipient now gives it to the only person in the room, or asks "Give the X to whom? (A or B)" and takes the answer ("seller", "to the seller", "the bald man") as the second half of the command.

**The pickpocket strikes only after a warning (found in play):** he used to strike two commands after the player arrived in the Bazaar, whatever they did, so a player who talked to the Merchant and then the bald man was robbed before the bald man's tip could be earned. Now there are two phases. **Stalking:** on arrival he appears ("a thin man in a stained waistcoat... drifts closer through the crowd with his eyes on your watch chain") but does not act. **The warning, then two turns:** the countdown starts when the player is warned, which happens in one of three ways: asking the bald man about the thief (even without a comb he now says "He has been at your back since you arrived, effendi, and his eyes are on that watch chain"), giving the bald man the comb (his full tip: show a blade openly), or, if the player has done neither, the thief closing in on his own after four turns ("The thin man is at your elbow now, close enough to smell his hair oil... You have perhaps two breaths"). After the warning the player has exactly two actions (a nudge at the first: "One breath"), and he strikes at the end of the second unless a repaired knife has been shown (`SHOW KNIFE`, +5) or the player has left the Bazaar (he never follows, and starts again on the next visit). A player who befriended the bald man earlier is warned the moment they arrive. The watch is still recoverable from the dead thief in the dunes.

**Distance and time in the desert (design note, after play):** the early desert felt immediate, so the rooms now say how long the journey is, and the player's watch measures it. Timings, all from the City Gate: the **Desert Road** is one hour out ("You have been travelling for an hour along this road and the heat is draining"; with the pocketwatch, "You check your pocketwatch: an hour out from the City Gate, to the minute"; without it, "you judge it by the sun and the ache in your thighs"), and "the oasis, the last well on the caravan road, lies a further two hours' ride to the west". The **Oasis** is three hours out (watch: "three hours since the City Gate"), and "from what you remember of the papyrus fragment" the **Shifting Dunes** lie about two hours' ride beyond it. The **Dunes** are five hours from Cairo ("You have been riding for two hours since the oasis. Your pocketwatch says five hours since Cairo."). The watch text only appears if the player still has it (the pickpocket may have taken it; the dead thief in the dunes returns it). There is no clock in the game; the times are fixed per place. Camp, ridge and valley have no timings yet.

**The waterskin is full from the start:** Whitfield fills it, so the Gate Guard's "water" is literally true. It begins `clean` (the water is warm and "smells of goat"; `DRINK WATER` says so). At the oasis the vendor takes the skin, sniffs the warm water, pours it away "with a tut of professional disgust" and refills it from his jug with cold clean water. In the Flooded Passage the full skin must be emptied before it can be filled with the black water (the first pour is clean water, "you wince as good water goes down a hole"), so the key puzzle is unchanged. Trying to fill the skin in Cairo now says it is full already.

**Three desert changes after play:** (1) **The oasis vendor** no longer has a moustache tied behind his neck (which duplicated the Bazaar's Bald Man): he has black hair down his whole back "with a sheen that would make an Arabian horse jealous", and still a cooking spoon behind his ear. (2) **Thirst.** Every action in the desert (the Desert Road, Oasis, Dunes, Camp, Ridge and Valley; looking, inventory, score and the like do not count) dries the player out, and drinking puts it right. At 12 actions: "The sun has found the back of your skull. Your tongue is thick in your mouth, and the horizon has begun, very gently, to sway. You ought to drink something." At 17: "The world tips, and rights itself, and tips again... Your waterskin hangs warm and heavy at your hip. Drink." At 21: "Your sight narrows to a tunnel with sparkling edges, and the reins have slipped almost out of your hand... If you do not drink now, she will choose her own way." At 24 the camel wanders onto loose sand and sinks, and the player comes to on the Desert Road ("the camel standing over you, looking, for the first time in your acquaintance, apologetic"), with the dune and ridge progress reset and nothing else lost. `DRINK`, `DRINK WATER` or `DRINK FROM THE WATERSKIN` resets it ("The world steadies, the horizon stops swaying, and the camel's ears swing back to attention"), as does the mint tea. The waterskin never runs dry; if the player has poured it out, the oasis vendor refills it ("shaking his glossy head at the waste"). Leaving the desert resets the clock. (3) **The ridge on a camel.** The umbrella route now reads: "Atop the camel, the winds pull at your umbrella like a rough sail, and almost instinctively the camel requires no additional guidance, and proceeds with an eerie certainty. Head down, she lengthens her stride over ground you will never be able to retrace... The camel slows, snorts, and carries you down into a bowl of still, hot air." The other ridge routes say "ride" and "the camel" instead of walking.

**The umbrella, the Bedouin's warning and the storm (desert polish, after play):** (1) **The umbrella is for shade.** Whitfield already said "I'm not expecting rain, but you should expect some sun"; now the object says "There is no rain in the desert, but a black dome that size would throw a very respectable circle of shade", and it works: `OPEN UMBRELLA` in any desert room gives "a small, warm circle of black shade... There is not a cloud in the sky, and it is the most useful thing you have done all day", and while it is up only every other action counts toward thirst. `CLOSE UMBRELLA` (or `FURL`) brings back the sun "like a slap". The first thirst reminder suggests it. It is still lost at the ridge whichever way the player crosses. (2) **The Bedouin warn about the ridge.** When he gives the mint tea the host looks west: "Do not go over the Sandstorm Ridge, guest. The winds there are treacherous, and a man cannot hold a direction in them. Even the finest compass lies to its master beyond the ridge." He shrugs: "Of course, you will go. Guests always do." The same speech answers `ASK HOST ABOUT THE RIDGE` (or the storm, the wind, the compass, the pyramid). It is the hint that the pyramid interferes with magnetic fields, and the compass now behaves accordingly: at the ridge it trembles, in the storm it "settles with great confidence on the south-west. You are quite sure that is wrong", and in the Valley "turns slowly, round and round, as though something beneath the sand were pulling at it" (matching the pillar wing). (3) **The crossing is remembered.** Both ways of crossing the ridge end with: "You check your pocketwatch with a shaking hand: you have been in the storm for an hour and a quarter" (or, without a watch, "By the angle of the light, you judge that you have been in the storm well over an hour"), then "You crossed it in near-blindness, but you saw things in the flying sand: fragments of skeletons, human and camel, half-buried and scoured pale; momentary glints of forgotten metal in the desert floor; and, among the rushing of the wind, whispers that were not quite voices. It is no surprise that so few have come this way, and that the pyramid is still a ghostly rumour."

**The oasis vendor no longer trades by himself (found in play):** `BUY DATES` handed over the wallet automatically (the swap verbs complete a barter), so the player was never asked to find what he wanted, and his answers gave it away. Now the vendor is a *prompt-style* trader (`prompt: true` in `tradeOffers`): `BUY`, `GET`, `TRADE` or `ASK ABOUT DATES` get "The finest dates in all of Egypt, my friend, and certainly the only dates at this oasis. Ha! Trade me something I might want." The player has to offer something (`GIVE`, `SWAP X FOR DATES` or `TRADE X`), and wrong offers get "Not that, my friend. Look at me. What does a man like me want?" The clue is `EXAMINE` him (or `TRADER`, `VENDOR`): "His shirt hangs open over a broad brown chest, on which someone has tattooed, in faded blue ink, an elephant with its trunk raised", which matches the silver elephant embossed on the Indian wallet. Asking him about trade or his bag gets "Look at me properly. What does a man like me love?"; only naming the wallet, the leather or the elephant gets "THAT. Give it to me, and the oasis is yours." The other vendors (the docks, the rug dealer, the Bedouin host) still complete a swap when the player uses a swap verb. Two small parser fixes came with this: `ASK ABOUT <topic>` with no person named means the only person in the room (or asks whom, if there are several), and `GET ALL` says "where they belong" for more than one scenery item.

**Dates, stones and the dune tests (reworked after play):** the vendor's basket now holds **six dates**, tracked one at a time (the inventory shows "Dates in the basket: 5" and "Date stones: 1"). `EAT DATE` eats one and leaves one stone: "After tasting one of the best dates in Egypt, you have a sticky date stone in your hand." (the first time also earns +10 "ate at the oasis"); a player who never eats can instead throw a date. In the dunes, `THROW STONE <direction>` or `THROW DATE <direction>` (also `TOSS`, `LOB`, `CHUCK`, or a bare `THROW N`) tests the ground; with no direction the game asks "In what direction?" and takes the next word as the answer ("north", "w", "to the north"). A safe throw is retrieved (the stone or date is picked up again: "You wade out, brush it off and come back: the ground holds"); a bad throw is stripped by the beetles and costs one stone or one date, once per slope. `DROP STONE`, `DROP DATE` or `DROP DATES` in the dunes is not a test: "The date stone lands at your own feet. Try harder." (nothing is lost); elsewhere dropping date stones says "You decide to hang on to your date stones." Throwing anywhere but the dunes is refused ("You think better of it"). If the basket runs out, asking the vendor about dates (or `GET DATES`) gives another basket of six. The dunes room text mentions the stone, the basket or the lack of both.

**Dates and stones survive a sink, and the vendor restocks you (found in play):** a player who sank in the dunes and came back found they had no dates to throw and no wallet to trade. The sink itself never touched the inventory; the cause was the old `EAT DATE`, which ate the whole basket at once into six stones, after which `BUY DATES` said "Nobody's selling that here." Now the basket is six dates eaten or thrown one at a time (see the dune-testing note), the stones and the date count are kept when the player sinks and returns to the Desert Road (only the position and the dune stage reset; ground already tested safe is remembered), and a player who has already traded the wallet never needs it again: `BUY DATES`, `GET DATES`, `TRADE VENDOR` or `ASK VENDOR ABOUT DATES` with an empty basket gives another basket of six ("The oasis does not run out of dates.").

**Luck is allowed in the dunes (revision of the testing rule):** the correct route (north, then west) now works whether or not the player has tested it. Only the wrong slopes sink the player (south, or west on the first screen, or north on the second), so testing with a stone or a date is the safe way to know, not a requirement. Untested success says so: "You step out onto the sand without trying it first, and, by some small miracle, it holds", and on the second screen "The sand you did not test holds, all the way, by what feels like sheer luck". A tested route says "You step out onto the ground you tested. It holds." A slope the player has already seen swallow a stone says "You step out onto the slope you watched swallow your stone. It lets go at once", and an untested wrong slope still says "You step out onto the sand without trying it first. For a moment it holds. Then the whole slope lets go." This replaces the earlier rule that only tested ground could be stepped onto. A player with no stones and no dates can now cross by luck.

**The second dune screen says the ground has changed:** its description now begins "The dunes have shifted again, as they do out here, and the way you came has already been smoothed away", which is the player's cue that what was safe on the first screen (north) is not safe on the second (west is). A player who sinks on the second screen and restarts is told by that line, not by the sink message, that the truth moved.

**The fortune-teller's flute scene is now a moment (rewritten from the author's text):** the first `TALK TO TELLER` gives her greeting ("Sit, sit! Tell me your future, and I will tell you whether it is correct."), then: "You half laugh, but before you can finish the other half of your laugh, she presses a finger to your lips. A half-shocked, half-surprised look flashes in her eyes. 'I already see some of your future.' She then presses a reed flute with three finger holes into your hand. 'Where you are going, you will need to ask snakes politely. Keep it.'" (+5 was given a flute, as before). The look that crosses her face implies she has seen something she did not expect: a seed for the tarot card she later asks to have returned, and for her warning about the wind.

**The Valley of Forgotten Stones is now a night to survive (redesign, from the author's brief):**
- **After the storm** (both routes) the aftermath adds that "it feels like many hours more, and the shadows are growing long", and "You think to check your compass, but its needle spun, wandered and sulked the whole way across. - **The valley** has the author's description ("hundreds of yards apart", "It's now becoming dark. Here the air is deathly still and much cooler... The camel's watering at the oasis (+10) no longer gates the entrance, which also removes any chance of being trapped in the valley (there is no way back).

**The German tin of meat (a trap inside the valley's night):** `EXAMINE TIN` shows the German army markings: "an eagle over HEERESVERPFLEGUNG, and a date: JULI 1925. It is, you reflect, aged to perfection." It is sealed: `OPEN TIN WITH KNIFE` (or just `OPEN TIN`) uses the little hooked tin-opener on the back of the penknife (it works even though the main blade is snapped); anything else "won't open a tin".
